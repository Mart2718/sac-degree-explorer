This is the same circle, but now the point can go all the way around. Watch it cross 90°, 180°, 270°, and land back at 360° — right on top of 0°. Where does the point sit at 200°? Which quarter of the circle is that?

Check your thinking

The teal mark is one radius, bent along the circle — that's what 1 radian actually means. Drag all the way around and try to count how many full radius-lengths fit before you land back at 0°/360°. You'll come up just short of a 7th one — that leftover sliver is exactly why a full circle is 2π (about 6.28) radians, not a clean whole number.
